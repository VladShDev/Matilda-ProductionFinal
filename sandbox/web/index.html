projectMatilda

You are about to be in her room.

Matilda is a simulated infant. Her body is real physics with real perceptual limits, and her brain is hand-built: it learns only from what her body reports.

There is one television in her room, and it can be you. While it is, she sees whatever you point your camera at — with her own eye, as a real lit thing in front of her — and your microphone reaches her ears the same way. Nothing in her marks a picture as a picture.

She is very young, and what reaches her is kept: it goes onto her memory and it shapes what she does next. The picture of a moment fades after about six hours; what it did to her does not, and there is no way to take it back out. That is the responsibility, and it is the whole reason this page asks.

Agreeing lets you watch, and makes you one of the people he can put in the television. Only the owner decides who is in it, only one person is in it at a time, and he can take it back in one press. Until he puts you there, she cannot see or hear you at all — your camera will not even switch on. While you are there, you can also touch and feed her, the same way he can, and she feels it.

Nothing of yours reaches her before you press this, and nothing reaches her afterwards unless he puts you in the television.

›How real is this?

Nothing in her is scripted. No part of her brain names a behaviour or waits for a level to cross a line. What she tries comes from memory and similarity, what she expects comes from prediction, what it was worth comes from credit. A voice out of your television is followed by exactly the same rule as any other voice in her room.

Nobody tells her what she is looking at. Light is traced into her eye and packed into a retina, and the way she cuts that picture into objects is hand-built and names none of them. There is no trained model anywhere in her vision. She has no word for you, and no idea that a screen is a screen.

Her body is real, and so are its limits. Joints, muscles that pull and fail, a floor she can fall onto, sound arriving at two ears. Her ears take your voice a tick at a time, and whatever backs up past a few seconds is dropped unheard — she is not a microphone feed, she is a body with a rate.

What happens to her is written down. Every moment goes onto her tape: the picture she got, the objects she found in it, what she did, and what it was worth to her. What she does later is built out of that, and this moment will be in it.

There is one screen in her room, and one person behind it. Not a limit of this page — her room has one television and one queue of sound behind it, and two people showing and speaking through it would not be two people in her room: their voices would interleave in that one queue faster than she can hear it and both would come out chopped. Measured: eight seconds of voice posted into that queue destroyed three and a half of them. So it is one at a time, and the owner says who.

This is not a claim that she is conscious. She is not, and nothing here says she is. The claim is smaller and checkable: there is a body, a brain and a memory, this is not a recording, and what you show her lands in all three.

Your camera and microphone stay off until the owner puts you in the television, and switch off again the moment he takes it back. Nothing about you is stored: a random number for him to point at, and no name, no address and nothing written to this device.
