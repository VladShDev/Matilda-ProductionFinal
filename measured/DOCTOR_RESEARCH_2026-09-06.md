# WHAT A DOCTOR CHECKS IN THE FIRST YEAR, AGAINST HER SHEET --- research, 2026-09-06

His ask, the night of 2026-09-06: *"find how doctors check kids ... complete all
our list with all needed tests"*. From the internet only (sources at the end):
the CDC milestone checklists (what about 75% of babies do by an age), the
newborn's primitive reflexes (StatPearls, Cleveland Clinic), the Hammersmith
Infant Neurological Examination (HINE, 16 items), and the AAP Bright Futures
visit schedule (3-5 days, 1, 2, 4, 6, 9, 12 months: measure, examine, watch
milestones, ask the parent).

Nothing here changes `DOCTOR.md`; that is his word. This is the list to
choose from. Every item names what the doctor does, what is read from
OUTSIDE her (never her chemistry), and whether her body can do it at all.

## The doctor's items by age, and where each stands with her

## The list, completed: what to add to the sheet, in the doctor's order

The ten items stand. These are the additions the doctor's own lists ask for
and her body can carry, cheapest first. Each is one script, with its control,
on his yes (rule 7).

11. **Moves both sides alike, smoothly** (HINE movement quantity and quality,
    the most predictive item at every age). A quiet minute, unswaddled. Read:
    spindle change per minute, left against right, and how jerky. Control:
    her own other minutes. YES today.
12. **Roots** (newborn). A touch at `mouth_left`, then `mouth_right`. Read: her
    neck turns to that side within a second. Control: no touch. YES today; she
    has no such reflex built, so the reading is what her trials found.
13. **Holds her head up when held** (4 months; ventral suspension, HINE). Her
    mother's lift, ten seconds. Read: the head above the trunk's line.
    Control: the first second of the lift. YES today; posture is unpaid.
14. **Looks at her own hands; brings hands to her mouth** (4 months). A quiet
    ten minutes unswaddled. Read: her gaze on a hand; a hand within reach of
    her mouth. Control: chance, from where her hands are anyway. YES today.
15. **Reacts when her mother leaves; lifts arms when she comes** (9 months).
    Read: her sounding, moving and arm height in the 20 s after, against
    the 20 s before. YES today.
16. **Peek-a-boo, and looks for what is hidden** (9, 12 months). The face on
    the screen shown and blanked; then the ball blanked. Read: her sounds at
    each return; her gaze staying where the ball was. Control: the blank
    alone. YES today.
17. **Takes turns; babbles strings** (6, 9 months). Item 8's visit, longer.
    Read: hers-mother-hers alternations; runs of one echo id. YES today.
18. **Turns to the sound her mother says most** (9 months, "her name").
    Read: the turn (as item 1) to that sound against a sound as loud she has
    heard as often from nowhere. YES today, no name in the code.
19. **Prone: head up, forearms, straight arms, rolls** (2, 4, 6 months).
    NEEDS A PROP: a hand that lays her on her front. Nothing pays for it yet.
20. **Sits with support, then alone; pulls to stand at the bars** (6, 9, 12
    months). NEEDS A PROP: a hand that sits her. Nothing pays for it yet.
21. **Protective reactions: arm protection, forward parachute** (9 months,
    HINE). NEEDS A PROP: a hand that tips her.

Not for her, and not on any sheet of hers: smiles, laughs, faces, the mirror,
fingers (grasp, pincer, banging, hand to hand), food, cup, container, waving,
pat-a-cake, stepping under a hand (the walker is off on his word).

Two things the research says about the items we already run:

- **Fixation is a human bar.** The CDC's "looks at a toy for several seconds"
  is the fovea's need. She has none. His call of 2026-09-06 to drop it stands
  with the sources: what the doctor is really asking is "does she notice it
  and watch it move", which are items 2 and 3 without the fixation line.
- **Nothing in the first year is passed in four minutes.** The doctor watches
  a quarter hour and asks the mother about the weeks. Item 4's first run was
  four minutes swaddled: a flow shorter than any doctor's.
